GapCVP.Comparator.finitePNormGapCVPIsNPHard

IdeaThe proof transfers NP-hardness across the bookkeeping change of representation that passes from the original finite $p$-norm GapCVP problem $\Pi_p$ to the comparator version $\Pi_p^{\mathrm{cmp}}$. The two agreement lemmas show that, bit string by bit string, the comparator's yes- and no-predicates coincide with the original ones; since NP-hardness is a property of the yes/no behaviour of a promise problem, the paper's unconditional NP-hardness theorem for $\Pi_p$ (valid for every rational $p\ge 1$) carries over verbatim to $\Pi_p^{\mathrm{cmp}}$. Note that the disjointness of the comparator's yes- and no-sets, which makes it a promise problem at all, is discharged inside the definition of $\Pi_p^{\mathrm{cmp}}$, not in this proof.

IdeaBoth promise problems express the same no-condition, one for comparator instances and one for GapCVP instances. GapCVP.Comparator.isNPHardPromise_of_original

IdeaThe proof is a direct transport of reductions along equivalences of promise conditions. Unfolding the decide-valued definition of NP-hardness turns NPHardPromise original = true into the statement that every NP language reduces to the original problem, and turns the goal into the corresponding statement for the comparator problem. Given an NP language $A$, the hardness of the original problem supplies a reduction $R$ from $A$ to it; the same map and the same polynomial-time machine give a reduction to the comparator problem, because its yes- and no-predicates agree pointwise with those of the original, so $R$'s completeness and soundness carry over verbatim through those equivalences.

Statement

Let $P$ be a GapCVP promise problem and $Q$ a comparator promise problem whose yes- and no-predicates agree pointwise with those of $P$, i.e. $Q.\mathrm{yes}(x)=\mathrm{true}$ iff $P.\mathrm{yes}(x)=\mathrm{true}$, and $Q.\mathrm{no}(x)=\mathrm{true}$ iff $P.\mathrm{no}(x)=\mathrm{true}$, for every bit string $x$. If $P$ is NP-hard, then $Q$ is NP-hard. Here a promise problem is a pair of Boolean predicates $\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{no}$ on bit strings (GapCVP.PromiseProblem, GapCVP.Comparator.PromiseProblem), NP-hardness of $P$ (GapCVP.NPHardPromise) means that every language $A\subseteq\{0,1\}^*$ in NP admits a promise reduction to $P$, and a promise reduction (GapCVP.PromiseReduction, GapCVP.Comparator.PromiseReduction) is a map $f$ on bit strings together with a polynomial-time Turing machine computing it (Nonempty (BitTM f)), such that $x\in A$ implies $P.\mathrm{yes}(f(x))=\mathrm{true}$ (completeness) and $x\notin A$ implies $P.\mathrm{no}(f(x))=\mathrm{true}$ (soundness). NP itself (GapCVP.IsNP) is the Boolean assertion that the language has a polynomial-time verifier with a polynomial bound on certificate length.

IdeaThe two conversions between a GapCVP instance and a comparator instance are both field-by-field copies of the same four data, so converting to the comparator representation and back is the identity by definitional equality, discharged by reflexivity.
